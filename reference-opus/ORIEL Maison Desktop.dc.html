The Maison · Since 1994

One atelier,
one address

ORIEL was founded in a first-floor apartment on rue Taitbout with one cutting table and two tailors. Thirty-two years later the table has not moved.

We make forty to sixty garments a week, in short series, for clients we mostly know by name. We publish our mills, our lead times and our repair policy because a house that cannot show its supply chain is asking to be trusted on decoration alone.

Savoir-faire

Eleven days, nine stages

Three of them have never been mechanised. They are the reason a coat keeps its line for decades rather than seasons.

I
Pattern drawn in kraft paper

Every style is redrawn each season rather than scaled from an archive block.

By hand
II
Cloth rested before cutting

Twenty-one days flat in the studio so the weave settles and the garment stops moving later.

21 days
III
Cut with a table knife

One layer at a time. Slower than a die, and the only way to follow the nap exactly.

By hand
IV
Canvas basted into the chest

Two days of work that decide how the shoulder falls for the life of the coat.

By hand
V
First fitting on the body

Never on a stand. What fails the fitting goes back to stage one.

Atelier
VI — IX
Buttonholes, lining, press, inspection

Silk buttonholes worked by hand, cupro lining set loose, three presses, and a final check by the tailor who began it.

4 stages
